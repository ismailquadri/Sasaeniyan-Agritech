# Sasaeniyan Agritech — Market Validation Brief

**Date:** 2026-10-09
**Wedge under validation:** Market access / offtake for Nigerian smallholder farmers
**Primary user:** Smallholder farmers (rural, low-literacy-skewed, feature-phone/shared-smartphone reality)
**Method:** Synthesis of 5 parallel desk-research tracks mining publicly available data (news, forums, social, govt releases, GSMA/World Bank/IFAD/FAO/NBS reports). All claims cited inline in the source tracks; confidence flagged where directional.

> **Caveat on sources:** agents could reach only *public* web data — not private Facebook groups or authenticated Instagram. Hard numbers lean on institutional reports (GSMA, World Bank, FAO, NBS, NCC); qualitative pain signals lean on Nairaland, news interviews, and viral social posts. Treat quoted farmer testimony as directional-but-vivid, and the institutional numbers as solid.

---

## 1. PROBLEM — Verdict: BURNING (with an honest nuance)

Market access / offtake is the pain Nigerian smallholders voice **most emotionally** and the one that most directly bleeds their income. It is **not** cleanly the single #1 constraint — development literature ranks **finance/credit** as co-equal or higher — but the two are **structurally fused**: no cash + no storage → farmer must take whatever the middleman offers today.

**Top pains (ranked by frequency/intensity):**
1. **Middlemen underpay at farm gate** — most-cited, most emotional. Farmgate routinely 20–50% below same-day market price; sesame surveys: "poor pricing" (70%) + "exploitative middlemen" (63%) led all constraints.
2. **Perishables rot → forced distress sales** — no cold chain; tomatoes spoil in days (40–65% loss).
3. **Harvest-glut price crashes** — tomato 50kg basket fell ~90% (₦140–150k → ₦13–15k).
4. **No buyers / collapsed offtakers** (Dangote Tomato, GBFoods shutdowns stranded farmers).
5. **Bad roads + transport + tout extortion** (transport up to ~60% of final price).
6. **No price transparency** — middlemen know real prices; farmers don't.
7. **Cash-strapped farmers must sell immediately to repay loans** → guaranteed low price.

**Representative voices:**
- *"Yesterday, I dumped the tomato I took to Gambarou market in the river because people refused to buy… the loss is unimaginable."* — Malam Ardo Modu, Borno (Daily Trust)
- *"I learnt it is sold for N6,000 a basket [in Port Harcourt]"* — Talatu Danjuma, who sells for a few hundred naira locally (Daily Trust)
- Nairaland maize farmers: when you hear the middleman's resale price *"you will shed tears."*
- Benue (Tiv) women forced to sell baskets at **₦50**, net ~₦30 after tax.

**Quantified:**
- **~38M smallholders** (~20% of population), producing **~90% of national food output.**
- **Post-harvest loss: ~30–50% of produce; ₦3.5–5 trillion (~$9–10bn)/yr.**
- **Middlemen capture 30–50% of retail (tomato margins ~70%).** Cowpea bought ₦250–300, resold ₦900–950 (~3×).
- Only **~31%** of even AGRA-supported farmers reach structured markets.

**Why farmers still use middlemen (the dependence trap):** middlemen are the *only* ones who come to the farm, pay **cash on the spot**, and **pre-finance** inputs (advances the farmer must sell back against). With no storage, no transport, and only ~7% getting bank loans, immediate cheap sale beats total loss. *"The middleman is purchasing urgency."*

**Why workarounds fail:** WhatsApp "zero-middleman" groups (scattered, logistics unsolved, scam risk); hauling to distant markets (transport + touts + spoilage); cooperatives (undercapitalized, bank-unrecognized); processor offtake (collapses when processors stop buying).

**Honest counter-evidence:** ~80% of farmers find finance "difficult/very difficult"; 78% financially excluded. Rigorous sources refuse to rank markets above credit, calling it interlocking "institutional failure." **One useful nuance:** on-farm tomato loss is <1% — losses concentrate at **transport/aggregation/market** stages, which *supports* an aggregation/offtake wedge over a "crops rot in the field" framing.

**→ Defensible claim:** *"Market access/offtake is the pain where smallholders lose the most **capturable income**, and the most **addressable** via aggregation + guaranteed offtake + price transparency — bundled with embedded finance."*

## 2. COMPETITION — The graveyard tells us how to win

**Failures / distress (the lessons):**
- **Twiga Foods (Kenya)** — raised ~$185m; broke on unit economics. A ~20% whole-chain markup can't cover logistics + QC + CAC + tech. **Pure commission marketplaces don't make money.**
- **AFEX** — in active contraction: turnover ↓~64% YoY, ~40% layoffs (795→478), EFCC/CBN probe, **₦17.8bn GTBank Anchor Borrowers default** (sector's defining solvency event). Model: warehouse-receipt / grain-credit-against-collateral.
- **FarmKonnect** — collapsed agro-crowdfunding; founder wanted over alleged ₦50M+ fraud. **Fixed-return retail crowdfunding is dead** (also killed/pivoted Farmcrowdy, ThriveAgric crisis).
- **Tradebuza** — probable 2025 shutdown.

**Working models:**
- **ColdHubs** — cleanest success. Asset-based, **pay-as-you-store** solar cold storage, 58 hubs, grant-funded, expanding.
- **Releaf** — captures value via **processing** (palm fruit → oil near farms), $100m+ supply contracts. Stacks margin.
- **Crop2Cash** — input-financing + **credit-scoring**, USSD rails (CashCard), marketplace/SupplyBase. Owns the farmer-finance rail.
- **Agricorp** — only verified sizeable raise ($17.5M, 2021); smallholder → processing → export (spices/high-value). 2024–26 health opaque.
- **Winich Farms** — asset-light aggregation playbook; sign the buyer first.

**⚠️ Warehouse-receipt convergence:** both AFEX and Hello Tractor landed on warehouse receipts as their market-access vehicle — the exact model at the center of AFEX's blowup. **Avoid grain-credit-against-collateral.**

**THE GAP WE ATTACK:**
1. **Own perishables/horticulture offtake, NOT saturated dry grains.** (Grains = crowded + warehouse-receipt death zone; perishables = burning pain + higher margin + less contested.)
2. **Solve side-selling with transparent, market-linked pricing** — not costly field armies.
3. **Fund via offtake-backed institutional debt, never retail crowdfunding. Sign the buyer FIRST; stay asset-light** (Winich's playbook, not Vendease's or AFEX's).

## 3. GOVERNMENT RAILS — The N-ATLAS correction + the real opportunity

**⚠️ CRITICAL CORRECTION:** **N-ATLAS is NOT an agriculture platform.** It is the *"Nigerian Atlas for Languages & AI at Scale"* — an open-source multilingual **LLM** (Yoruba/Hausa/Igbo/Pidgin-English), launched 29 Sept 2025 by the Ministry of Communications & Digital Economy. No farmer database, no land/logistics, no market linkage.
→ **Legitimate use:** a *future local-language voice/chatbot layer* for farmer UX — a feature, not a backbone.

**The platform we actually want:**
- **🎯 National Digital Farmers Registry (NDFR)** — launched **Nov 2025** by Federal Ministry of Agriculture (FG–IFAD plan). NIN-verified + **geo-tagged farmers & farmlands**, built explicitly for "credit, insurance, **market information**." Portal: ndfrplatform.ng. Has a **Multi-Stakeholder Platform to onboard private sector** (same door IFAD/OCP used). **Crucially: NDFR stops at *identity* — it does NOT solve offtake. That is our gap.**
- **NAPS** — AI + satellite land/crop mapping, yield forecasts, production-risk scores (federal/state access only so far).
- **NEEP** — National e-extension platform, targets 5M farmers/yr, multilingual, onboards **financial institutions**.
- **NADF / FISP / NAGS-AP** — federal ag-financing + ready-made, verified, offtake-aligned farmer cohorts (e.g., NAGS-AP: 80,000 farmers / 40,000 ha dry-season wheat).

**Access reality:** partnership/MoU-driven; **no public APIs or developer docs yet**; newly launched, piloting, politically exposed.

**→ How we harness it:** Position govt integration as **upside/moat, NOT dependency.** Our model works on our own aggregated farmer base today; NDFR compounds it. Pitch line: *"Government verifies and geo-tags farmers (NDFR); we turn that verified base into guaranteed income through market access."* Credentialing: *"Aligned with NATIP 2022–2027, NDFR, NADF/FISP, NAGS-AP."*

## 4. USER CONTEXT — Ground truth that dictates the build

Smallholders are rural, Northern-skewed, lower-income, ~40% female — the hardest-to-reach segment. National averages flatter them; use rural/Northern baselines.

- **Phones:** large share on **feature phones**; rural smartphone ownership low (nationally only 47% of women own an internet-enabled phone; 16% no phone).
- **Internet:** rural internet use just **23%** (vs 57% urban); **~39% of connections are 2G**, ~82M Nigerians on 2G/3G; rural 4G <50%.
- **Literacy:** national 60.9%; **rural 54% (F 48%)**; North-West **45%**, North-East 48%. Many Northern women farmers cannot read English menus.
- **Language:** Hausa (~63M, the farming belt), Pidgin (~60M lingua franca), Yoruba (~47M), Igbo (~46M). **English-only is a non-starter.**
- **USSD:** the backbone of rural finance — works on 2G voice, no data, no smartphone (₦4.84tn in USSD txns in 2023). **Reaches people an app never will.**
- **Gender gap:** women 35% mobile-internet use vs men 52%; entry handset = 24% of a woman's monthly income.
- **Power:** rural grid access **26%**; assume devices frequently off/low-battery, charging is paid-for.
- **Money/trust:** 18% lack mobile-money accounts (no ID); failed-USSD history eroded trust → receipts matter.

**HARD DESIGN CONSTRAINTS:**
- Must work on **2G**; no feature depends on 4G/reliable data.
- **USSD/SMS is the PRIMARY farmer channel, not a fallback.** Core flows (register, prices, offers, confirmations) must complete via `*code#` on a feature phone with zero data.
- **PWA = the agent/co-op/buyer tier** (smartphone, data-literate), not the mass-farmer tier.
- **Vernacular-first:** Hausa + Pidgin mandatory; Yoruba/Igbo by region; default by geography. Voice/IVR + icon UI for low literacy.
- **Offline-first with sync**; minimize data payload & battery draw.
- **SMS confirmation/receipt for every transaction** (trust + readable without data).
- Design for **shared & basic devices, and for women**: short, resumable sessions; agent-assisted / no-ID onboarding paths.

## 5. MARKET SIZE & BUSINESS MODEL

- **TAM:** ~**$233bn / €222bn** Nigerian food market by 2026.
- **SAM:** 70–80% of food flows through informal markets ≈ **$160–185bn** aggregatable GMV.
- **SOM (5-yr):** at a credible **1–3% take rate** on $300–500m intermediated GMV = **$3–15m net revenue** — grounded, not hockey-stick.
- **Money left on table:** ₦3.5–5tn/yr post-harvest loss; middlemen capture 30–70% of retail.

**Monetization — must STACK margin (commission alone fails):**
1. Transaction take rate (1–3%) — necessary, not sufficient.
2. **Embedded finance** (input credit, offtake advances) — spread + solves the distress-sale trap.
3. **Input margin** (sell quality inputs into the same relationship).
4. **Logistics/aggregation fee.**
5. **Data/SaaS** (buyer sourcing, traceability, credit-scoring) — highest margin.
6. Optional later: **light processing** for high value-to-weight crops.

**Investor context:** African agritech $206.9m (2024) → $168.1m (2025) — disciplined reset, not collapse; developing-market agrifoodtech **+63%**. Investors reward **net revenue + contribution margin per farmer/tonne + retention, not GMV.** Target 15–30% gross margin (logistics-heavy), 30–40%+ (SaaS/data).

**Tailwinds:** food inflation 19.57%; population → 450m by 2050 (staple demand +50%); NATIP 2022–27 / NASIP 2026 fund aggregation/storage/digital-ag; AfCFTA export market; agri-input market ~$5.7bn.

**PITCH-READY NUMBERS:**
1. **$233bn** food market (TAM).
2. **70–80%** via informal markets ≈ **$160–185bn** aggregatable (SAM).
3. **₦3.5–5tn ($2–10bn)/yr** post-harvest losses.
4. **30–70%** of retail captured by middlemen — the margin we redistribute.
5. **80–88%** of farmers are smallholders producing **~90%** of output; agriculture = **26% of GDP**.
6. **1–3%** proven take rate → monetize on stacked revenue, not GMV.
7. **$168m** African agritech (2025); developing-market agrifoodtech **+63%**.
8. **450m** population by 2050; staple demand **+50%**.

## 6. STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS (what the data forces)

1. **Problem statement must bundle offtake + the distress-sale/finance trap** — not "market access" alone.
2. **Focus on perishables/horticulture** (tomato, pepper, onion) — burning pain, higher margin, avoids the crowded + dangerous grain/warehouse-receipt zone.
3. **Demand-first, asset-light:** sign buyers (processors, large traders, institutional) *before* mobilizing supply. Guaranteed offtake is the hook that beats the middleman.
4. **Price transparency is the trust wedge** against side-selling — cheaper than field armies.
5. **Channel-split architecture:** USSD/SMS (+IVR, vernacular) for farmers; PWA for agents/co-ops/buyers. This *is* the inclusion story investors reward.
6. **Govt rails = moat, not dependency.** NDFR identity + our offtake layer = clean integration narrative; works without it.
7. **Monetization must stack margin** from day one in the model (embedded finance + inputs + data), even if the demo showcases the core marketplace flow.
8. **N-ATLAS** reappears correctly as the **local-language voice layer** — a credible, on-trend AI feature, not the backbone.
